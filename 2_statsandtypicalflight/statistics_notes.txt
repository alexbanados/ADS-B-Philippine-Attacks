Raw latitude and longitude were retained as positional information, but they were not used directly 
to determine attack intensity. Since latitude and longitude naturally vary over the route, 
their raw standard deviation mainly reflects route distance rather than abnormal positional variation. 
Therefore, position-based attacks were defined in meter-scale offsets using derived spatial features 
such as distance per timestep, cumulative distance, and route progress.
